# Labelling the cases

Three labellers, each a sub-agent that read the 55 standards in full and never saw what the cases were designed to cover, labelled the 30 cases independently: `labeller-a.json`, `labeller-b.json`, `labeller-c.json`. Where all three agreed (327 labels), that is the label. The 63 disagreements were settled by David, mostly by rules that were then applied to every case, voted or not. [../expected.json](../expected.json) is the result: each expected standard with its reason and its source, `unanimous` or `decision N` below.

After labelling, C21's diff was corrected to show the `Appointment` model as context instead of as added lines; it never changed, as the case already said, and no label depended on it.

## Decisions on the disagreements (David, 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03)


1. integration-logic applies to any code that talks to a database: a database is an application too. Applied to every code case with database access, including those where no labeller voted it.
2. leave-nothing-running applies when the role's work requires starting something that keeps running (a dev server, a container, a worker, a browser, a tunnel). C12 and C23: applies. C05, C10, C20: does not.
3. bounded-context-layout applies when the change creates or moves server-side source files, well placed or not. C20, C23, C24: applies. C19 (only a Next.js route, placed by the framework's routing): does not.
4. repository-no-business-logic applies to any change that writes or edits a repository method, even pure field mapping. C11, C14, C17: applies.
5. When a case writes, moves or reviews a business-logic entry point, every standard of the entry-point family whose own condition holds applies, voted or not: always one-per-module, prefer-top-level-functions, command-query-separation, cqs-handler-signatures, colocate-types, primitive-input-types, execution-context and typical-domain-entity-entry-points; with a database, database-transaction and transaction-isolation-levels; when it stores or reads entities, use-repositories, repository-interface and repository-domain-types; when it returns entity data, domain-entity-payload-types; when its caller must be authenticated, ensure-authenticated-requester and ensure-authorized-requester (C28 yes; C23, a system job, no).
6. pre-merge-gates does not apply to the reviewer: its scope is every route into the main branch, the act of integrating, and the reviewer integrates nothing. C18, C21: does not. (Observation: if reviewers should check gates 4–6 were met, that belongs in the reviewer's role in Shipping Increments, not in this standard's scope.)
7. expand-contract-schema-changes applies to any change to what is stored, additive ones included: its scope is anything stored, and its report (destructive or not, which step, the repository's check) is required of every such change. C12, C15: applies. C12 is therefore not a near miss for it, as it was designed to be.
   (Reconfirmed by David after discussing it: kept as the standard is written.)
8. prefer-named-functions applies when the change defines a function, inline callbacks included, even where the standard's own idiom exception lets them stay anonymous. C25 (inline test callbacks): applies. C11 (only lines inside existing named functions change): does not.
9. domain-service applies in C13: a domain service cannot hold logic that only one aggregate needs — that is a function of the aggregate — and that rule is written only in domain-service (goal, rule 6, detection). C13 is therefore not a near miss for it. Finding: the standard's scope excludes "logic within the same aggregate", contradicting its own rule 6; its scope must include defining a domain service, or anything named or shaped as one, whose logic involves a single aggregate.
10. business-logic-ensure-business-constraints follows its scope: any precondition or required-state check guarding a business operation, in an entity or an entry point, and any business rule extracted into a helper. C13 (markAsPaid on a non-issued invoice), C18 (authentication, authorization, checkWhetherInvoiceCanBeVoided): applies.
11. utc-zoned-temporal-types follows its scope: defining, parsing, serializing, persisting or converting zone-aware values. C14 (parses startsAt as an Instant, converts with toZonedDateTimeISO): applies. C18 (only Instant arithmetic between existing values): does not.
12. Settled together, each by its standard's scope: C17 typical-domain-entity-entry-points yes (rule 5); C19 business-logic yes; C30 business-logic yes; C19 domain-entity-uuidv4-ids yes; C30 domain-entity-uuidv4-ids yes; C26 domain-entity-reference-direction yes; C29 domain-entity, immutable-domain-entities, failure-reporting and internationalization yes; C14 failure-reporting no (nothing decides to emit to a tracker, an alert or an error log; making the race's loser an expected error is its error type, which the standard leaves out).
